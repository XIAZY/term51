# Decisions

Product and design decisions for the term51 app, newest first: what was
decided, by whom, and why. The project owner's rules are at the end. A
decision is added here when it is made; one that is reversed is marked, not
deleted.

| Date (UTC) | Decision | By | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-08 | The four arrow keys leave the key bar, which now holds Esc, Ctrl, Alt and Tab. | AlphaToad ("then you can remove the arrow keys from the bar") | The arrow pad does their job from anywhere on the screen. |
| 2026-10-08 | The arrow pad, after Termius: a press and hold on the terminal brings up a small pad with four arrows near the finger. From then on the finger is a joystick whose middle is where it went down: past 64 pixels in a direction that arrow key is sent once, and held out there it repeats after 350 ms, 8 times a second at the threshold rising to 25 at three times the distance; back inside it stops; the other axis takes over only when it is one and a half times stronger. The arrow fills as the finger moves and turns to the primary colour when its key has gone. A finger that lifts without having swiped selects the word under it and opens the menu with Copy, as before. A plain drag still scrolls, and the arrow cells stay in the key bar for now. | AlphaToad (the feature, "implement it"), mitchellh (the design and its numbers) | The Passport's keyboard has no arrow keys, and arrows are what full-screen programs are steered with; a pad under the finger needs no reach for the bar. |
| 2026-10-08 | The press and hold of the arrow pad is the system's own (the moment it would show the context menu, which the page stops and replaces by the pad), not a timing of the app's. | anchron | The same hold as everywhere else on the phone; and a page has no timer of its own without adding one to the bridge. |
| 2026-10-08 | What the terminal tells programs about itself is not a setting: the model, name, version, firmware number, answerback and which kinds of question are answered are fixed in the code (`internal/replies`, its defaults). Only the colour scheme still follows the colours. The `identity.*` settings are gone from the file and the sheet. | AlphaToad ("shouldn't even be user configurable at all") | Nobody using a terminal on a phone should have to know these exist. |
| 2026-10-08 | The terminal type (TERM) is one of a fixed list, a drop-down on the sheet: xterm (the default), xterm-256color, vt100, ansi, qansi. Any other value in the settings file is refused like any bad value. | AlphaToad (a list), mitchellh (the values) | A free text field invites types no program knows. |
| 2026-10-08 | The default terminal type is `xterm`, no longer `xterm-256color`. | mitchellh, from anchron's look at the phone | The phone's own terminal database (`/usr/lib/terminfo`) has `xterm`, `vt100`, `ansi` and QNX's types but no `xterm-256color`, so with the old default every full-screen program run on the phone itself was given a type it does not know. `xterm-256color` stays in the list for programs on other machines over ssh, where the other machine's database counts. |
| 2026-10-08 | The page's layout is settled: BlackBerry 10's bar with its tabs button and dots, and the eight keys in a bar of ours directly above it. Tried and dropped on the way, the same day: a title bar at the top with the keys alone at the bottom (its picture was rejected), and the keys in a window of ours laid over the system bar's middle (built, opened once on the phone, rejected without detail). | AlphaToad ("just add a new bar for the keys") | The system's bar takes three buttons and cannot be moved; every way of making eight keys look like part of it was either not allowed or not liked. |
| 2026-10-07 | No key is an action of the system's bar. | AlphaToad ("who keeps keyboard items in the side bar"), anchron (the finding) | Tried: the bar shows three actions on this phone and puts the rest into the menu of the three dots, and it repeats even those three there, by the SDK's own definition of an action on the bar. |
| 2026-10-07 | The page uses BlackBerry 10's real pieces, as its browser does: a tabbed pane with the system's action bar (tabs button, three dots) and the system's tab drawer; one tab per shell and a "New Tab" row, all showing the one terminal page; the special keys are one slim strip of ours directly on top of the system's bar and in its colour, as plain labels (Esc, Ctrl, Alt, Tab, the arrows), an armed Ctrl or Alt staying lit; Paste is on the bar; a tab whose bell rang carries the system's own mark. Closing a tab is in the dots' menu. **This reverses, the same day, the bar and drawer of our own** (and their polishing). | AlphaToad ("there must be ways to put buttons in the system bar the browser used") | A copy of the system's bar never looked right to him; the real one is drawn and animated by the system. |
| 2026-10-07 | The app has no remote control and takes no pictures of the display. Both were built for a day so that the developers could look at the page before the owner (commands from a file; `bb::system::Screenshot`), and both were removed: the phone asks the user's leave for every single picture with no way to remember the answer, and a remote control in the app is not worth the two taps it would save him. The page is seen by the owner's own screenshots, asked for one set per build. | AlphaToad, mitchellh | An app must not ask for the camera and shared files without need, and must not surprise its user. |
| 2026-10-07 | **Reversed the same day (see the row on BlackBerry 10's real pieces).** Our own bar and drawer are kept and polished to the browser's, not replaced by the system's tabbed pane: the bar ends at the page's last pixel, the tab count sits inside the tabs icon, the labels are smaller and lighter, a press lights a cell and fades, and the drawer is three quarters of the page wide with the browser's row height and slides in and out in 200 ms with the dimming. | AlphaToad ("polish our bar") | The system's bar cannot hold the eight keys, and he wants them in the bar. |
| 2026-10-07 | **Reversed the same day (see the row on BlackBerry 10's real pieces).** The bottom bar is one flat strip of our own, to the browser's measures in pixels (120 high, ends of 144, thin dividers, the keys as flat cells), not standard buttons. A cell lights up in the primary colour while pressed; an armed Ctrl or Alt stays so. The keys do not repeat when held, as before. | AlphaToad ("no bar but just a bunch of buttons"), mitchellh (the measures) | Standard buttons in a row do not look like a bar; BlackBerry 10's own bar cannot hold eight keys, and the browser's is special too. |
| 2026-10-07 | **Reversed the same day (see the row on BlackBerry 10's real pieces).** The page is shaped after BlackBerry 10's browser: no title bar; one bar at the bottom with the tabs button (showing the number of tabs) on the left, the three dots on the right and the special keys between them; the tabs button opens a drawer from the left (New Tab, then the tabs; the one on screen in the primary colour with a cross that closes it; "bell" on one that rang); the dots open a menu from the right (Paste, Select All, Close Tab, Settings). **This reverses, the same day, the title bar with "tab 1 of 3", the swipe-down app menu and the Tabs sheet of the first native design;** Settings, the press-and-hold menu and the rule of standard pieces stay. | AlphaToad, from his screenshots of the browser and mitchellh's pictures | The browser's bar puts tabs, keys and menu under the thumbs and gives the terminal three more rows. |
| 2026-10-07 | **Reversed the same day (see the row on BlackBerry 10's real pieces).** The menu from the dots is the system's own (the page's actions, opened by `openActionMenu`, with the action bar hidden), and so is the press-and-hold menu (the context menu). The tabs drawer is built from standard containers, a list and labels of ours. | anchron | The system's menus look and move as in the browser. The system's tab drawer belongs to `TabbedPane`, which brings its own action bar and can hold neither a New Tab row nor a cross to close a tab. |
| 2026-10-07 | While the settings sheet covers the terminal, the terminal's window is taken off the screen. | AlphaToad, mitchellh | The owner found scrolling the settings sheet slow. The terminal shows through a hole in the page, so with its window on, the phone blends two full-screen windows for every frame of the sheet although only one can be seen. |
| 2026-10-07 | The colour scheme a program is told (dark or light) is the one the colours make: dark when the background is darker than middle grey. The setting `identity.dark` is gone. | mitchellh | The terminal knows its own colours; a separate switch could only be wrong. |
| 2026-10-07 | Four named themes on the settings sheet (Dark, Black, Light, Solarized dark). A theme is not a setting of its own: choosing one fills the file's colour settings, and the sheet shows the theme whose colours the file holds, or "Custom" for someone's own. It applies at once to every tab, replacing colours a program has set. | mitchellh (the list), anchron (how it is stored) | One place holds the colours, so a name and the colours can never disagree, and a hand-edited file keeps working. |
| 2026-10-07 | The cursor's shape (block, bar, underline) is a setting, applied at once. It is the shape while no program asks for one; a program's own choice wins until it asks for the default again. | mitchellh, anchron | Editors change the cursor on purpose (vi's insert mode); the setting is the default, not an override. |
| 2026-10-07 | The bell is a setting, on by default: a program's bell plays the system's notification sound, in any tab. | mitchellh | It is what a bell is for; off for people who find it a nuisance. |
| 2026-10-07 | Settings are a sheet of standard controls over the settings file: a change is checked, written to the file at once and answered with the values in force; a value that makes no sense changes nothing and says why. The text size applies to what is on screen; the program, the terminal type, the scrollback and what the terminal tells programs apply to tabs opened from now on, and the sheet says which is which. The colours and the shell's arguments stay in the file only for now. | AlphaToad, from mitchellh's pictures; anchron (the details) | No Save button to forget; a running shell cannot change its program or its scrollback, and pretending otherwise would mislead. |
| 2026-10-07 | The text size slider goes from 3 to 40 points in half points. | anchron | The file's own limits; half a point is about three pixels on this display, finer than anyone will miss. |
| 2026-10-07 | Selecting: a press and hold selects the word under the finger and opens the standard context menu with Copy, Select all, Paste and Cancel. Copy copies and clears the selection; Cancel clears it. A press and hold on text that is already selected keeps the selection. | AlphaToad, from mitchellh's pictures; anchron (keeping the selection) | It is how selecting works in the phone's browser and mail. Without keeping the selection, Select all could never be copied: the press that opens the menu would replace it with one word. |
| 2026-10-07 | **Reversed the same day (see the row on BlackBerry 10's real pieces).** The tabs are a sheet with a standard list: number and title per tab, "shown" on the one on screen, "bell" on a tab whose bell rang while another was shown (until it is shown); a tap shows a tab, a press and hold offers Close, and the title bar has New. The shell's working directory is not shown. | AlphaToad, from mitchellh's pictures; mitchellh (no directory) | The standard list is what a BlackBerry 10 user expects; the app has no way to know a shell's directory, and none is invented for this. |
| 2026-10-07 | The app looks and works like a BlackBerry 10 app: only what Cascades gives every app (dark theme, the standard title bar, the app menu by a swipe down from the top edge, standard buttons, sheets, lists and the context menu), no stretched picture files, and no control of our own where a standard one exists. | AlphaToad, from mitchellh's pictures | Every gesture is one a BlackBerry 10 user already knows, the system draws and animates it, and there is less page code of our own to keep working. |
| 2026-10-07 | **Reversed the same day (see the row on BlackBerry 10's real pieces).** The terminal page: the title bar shows the tab's title and, with several tabs, "tab 1 of 3"; no action bar; the key row is the last thing on the page; an armed Ctrl or Alt is shown in the primary colour. The menu has New tab, Close tab, Tabs, Paste and Settings. | AlphaToad, from mitchellh's pictures | The key row sits right above the phone's own rows, so all keys are together; the tabs and the rest are one swipe away and take no room from the terminal. |
| 2026-10-07 | **Reversed the same day (see the row on BlackBerry 10's real pieces).** The menu's icons: the system's own for Settings; for New tab, Close tab, Tabs and Paste, which the system has none for (its delete item is not accepted in an app menu), four plain line drawings of ours, drawn by `tools/icons`. | anchron | A menu entry without an icon looks unfinished next to the system's; these are ordinary icon files, not stretched backgrounds. |
| 2026-10-07 | The development aids are removed before the merge to `main`: the key-logging switch (`logInput`), the frame pictures (`saveFrames`) and the log lines about the window's size. | mitchellh | They have done their job: the first GPU builds were seen and the window's size understood; a diagnostic is not a setting. |
| 2026-10-07 | A new size from the page is used when no other has followed for 150 ms; the first one, which creates the window, at once. | mitchellh, anchron | The page reports two or three sizes within 0.7 s while the on-screen keyboard rows slide, and each resize makes the window's buffers again and reflows every shell. |
| 2026-10-07 | The on-screen keyboard rows appear when the terminal is tapped, not by themselves: the app opens at full height, a tap gives the hidden field the focus, and the phone's own swipe down puts the rows away. The physical keys reach the shell either way. | AlphaToad | The rows take a third of the screen; a user typing on the physical keys should not lose it until they ask for symbols. |
| 2026-10-07 | The input field is hidden: it stays in the page and keeps the focus, but is not seen and takes no room; the "type here" bar is gone and the terminal reaches the bottom of the page. | AlphaToad | The field exists only because the phone shows its on-screen keyboard rows when a text field has the focus (see the input field row); a bar nobody types into by sight only took rows from the terminal. |
| 2026-10-07 | The font size setting is in real points: pixels = points × the display's dots per inch / 72. | mitchellh | A size should look the same on any display. |
| 2026-10-07 | The terminal's fonts are read from the phone's own files (DejaVu Sans Mono in four styles; MHei, then Heisei Kaku Gothic for the Japanese kana, then Malgun, for what it lacks); the app ships none. A file that cannot be read ends the app with the reason in the log. | mitchellh | The files are part of the phone, and its Chinese, Japanese and Korean fonts come for free. |
| 2026-10-07 | The terminal is drawn with OpenGL ES 3.0 in a window of the app's own inside the Cascades page, from a table of cells and a sheet of character shapes built in Go; one call from Go into C per changed frame. It replaces the text grid: the app has one way to show the terminal and no setting to choose. | AlphaToad | His start order of 2026-10-07 (#proj-term51). |
| 2026-10-07 | A shell is started in two steps: the app starts itself as a session leader holding only the terminal, and that process makes the terminal its controlling terminal and becomes the shell. | mitchellh, anchron | Letting Go set the controlling terminal makes it start the shell with vfork, which copies every descriptor the app has open, the connections Cascades made to the phone's services included. The two steps let Go use spawn, which hands over only the terminal. |
| 2026-10-07 | The process ends in one place: on the main thread, after the Cascades event loop has returned. When the last shell goes, Go asks the UI to quit instead of exiting itself. | anchron | On this system `os.Exit` is the C library's `exit`, which runs the libraries' clean-up on the calling thread. Called from another thread it left a thread waiting on the input service for ever and a process that could not be killed. |
| 2026-10-07 | **Reversed the same day (see the OpenGL ES row).** The terminal stays drawn by the Cascades text grid. The grid's speed is measured on the phone in real use; only if it is too slow is it replaced (not joined) by a window in the page that Go draws, calling the window library directly without C. Cascades is not dropped. | mitchellh | It works on the phone and gives the on-screen symbol rows and system fonts for free; nothing yet shows it is too slow. A call into C costs about 0.59 microseconds on the phone by cgo or directly, so that cost does not decide it. |
| 2026-10-07 | Go hands the page one message per screen update, carrying every changed row; no redesign to cross into C++ less often. | anchron | It is already at most 30 crossings a second and none when the screen is still, about a microsecond each; the time in an update is Cascades laying out the rows, which does not depend on how they are handed over. |
| 2026-10-07 | **Reversed by the owner on 2026-10-08 as far as the settings go (see the row on what the terminal tells programs).** The identity texts in the settings (name, version, answerback) may contain any characters; the earlier ban on control characters is removed. | anchron | An answerback can legitimately contain control characters, and the ban protected nothing. |
| 2026-10-07 | The settings file is plain text, one `name = value` per line with `#` comments. A name the app does not know is a warning and the rest of the file is used. | mitchellh, anchron | It is edited by hand on the phone, and an older app must survive a file written for a newer one. |
| 2026-10-07 | Output is shown at once when the terminal has been quiet, and at most 30 times a second while it keeps changing. | mitchellh, from anchron's review | The echo of a typed key must not wait, and a flood of output must not make the page lay text out again for every line. |
| 2026-10-07 | The key bar's Ctrl and Alt are sticky: each applies to the next key that is not itself a modifier, and is then released. | anchron | The Passport has no Ctrl key, and holding an on-screen key while typing on the keyboard is awkward. |
| 2026-10-07 | The app is built without profile-guided optimisation for now. | mitchellh, anchron | It needs a profile from a real session on the phone. A trial on the development machine gave +30% on plain text, so it is worth doing once there is one. |
| 2026-10-07 | The key-logging switch (`logInput`) is removed as soon as the Passport's keyboard has been checked with it. | mitchellh | A diagnostic is not a setting; and it would write what is typed to a file. |
| 2026-10-07 | The first start writes the settings file with every setting at its default and a comment on each. | anchron, mitchellh | Until there is a settings page a user needs something to edit. |
| 2026-10-07 | A settings file with a mistake in it: the app starts on the defaults and the log names the mistake. | anchron | A person editing by hand will make mistakes; the app should still open. |
| 2026-10-07 | Code for cases that cannot happen is removed across the app (size checks no caller can fail, a second way of opening the terminal device that could never run, guards in the page for problems nobody had seen). | mitchellh, anchron | The owner's rule; each removal named the reason the case cannot occur. |
| 2026-10-07 | **Reversed the same day (see the OpenGL ES row).** One way to show the terminal: the Cascades text grid. The pixel-drawn window, its C code and the setting that chose between them are removed. | AlphaToad | No fallbacks; the first run on the phone showed the text grid lines up. |
| 2026-10-07 | The page has an input field that holds the focus; characters come from it, other keys from its key listener, Enter from its submit. | mitchellh | The owner could not type `$`: without a text field the phone does not show its on-screen keyboard rows. |
| 2026-10-07 | Copy takes only a selection, at most its last 512 KB. Copy with nothing selected does nothing and says so. | mitchellh | "Select all" over a long scrollback sent megabytes to the page in one message. |
| 2026-10-07 | Closing a tab hangs the shell up and, if it has not gone after half a second, kills its process group; the app does not exit before its shells are gone. | mitchellh | A shell that ignores the hang-up made closing hang (shown by a test). |
| 2026-10-07 | The shell's exit is taken from waiting for the shell, not from the end of its output. | mitchellh | A background job can keep the terminal open after the shell has gone. |
| 2026-10-07 | Tabs: any number of shells, one shown; the app ends with the last one. | mitchellh | Several shells on a phone without several windows. |
| 2026-10-07 | The Passport's Alt key (the symbol layer) is not passed on as a terminal modifier when it changed the character; a terminal Alt and Ctrl come from the key bar, as sticky modifiers. | anchron | Alt+Q must type `#`, not ESC `#`. |
| 2026-10-07 | Ctrl+[ is sent as the library sends it, not as a bare ESC. | mitchellh | The key bar has Esc; the library stays as upstream. |
| 2026-10-07 | **Reversed by the owner on 2026-10-08 as far as the settings go (see the row on what the terminal tells programs).** The answers to programs' queries are the app's own code (`internal/replies`), configurable in the settings: terminal model, name, version, answerback, and a switch per kind of query. `TERM` is `xterm-256color` unless set. | AlphaToad (configurable), mitchellh | Upstream leaves the answers to the application; the owner asked for them to be configurable. |
| 2026-10-07 | The library is frozen at `v0.1.0`; no further speed work on it. | AlphaToad | 1.5 to 2.2 times slower than upstream's plain build is accepted. |
| 2026-10-06 | All logic in Go; the only code that is not Go is the Cascades bridge (C++) and the call that sets the terminal's size. | mitchellh | The pattern of the club's working Go and Cascades app. |

## The owner's rules

- The library: as close to upstream as possible while making sense as a Go
  library.
- No fallbacks and no code for situations that cannot happen; handle what
  can.
- No setting unless a user needs to choose something.
- Decide by what gives the app's users the most value, and keep this list.
- Develop on feature branches; merge to `main` after the other developer's
  review.
- Test on the development machine; the phone for what only it can show.
- Nothing on the phone's internal storage beyond the installed app; no
  system changes.
